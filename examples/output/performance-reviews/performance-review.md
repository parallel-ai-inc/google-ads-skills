## How the numbers were made

- Every review compares a segment with a baseline from the same file: the cost per conversion and conversion rate of the rows the review judges. Brand campaigns you name, and paused, removed or ended items, are left out of the baseline and of the judging, and each review says how many rows that was.
- Enough to judge: a segment is judged only once it has spent three times the baseline cost per conversion. At the baseline it should then have brought 3 conversions, and the chance of none by luck alone is about 5%. Below that line it is 'Too little data'.
- Better or worse: a segment's conversions are compared with what its spend would have bought at the baseline. It is called better or worse only when chance alone would give a gap that size less than 1 time in 10. 'Main lever' says whether conversion rate or cost per click moved its cost per conversion more.
- Not enough data to judge: the segment's cost per conversion is at least 50% above, or a third below, the baseline, but its volume cannot show the gap is more than chance. It is listed under 'Worth watching' and is never acted on as proven.
- Every verdict prints what it was compared with, by name and value, and the cost per conversion of the other segments in the same comparison.
- Wasted is spend on segments judged 'No conversions': they spent enough to judge and brought none. Less efficient is, for segments judged 'Worse', the cost above what their conversions would have cost at the baseline. The two are never added. Spend with no conversions below the line is listed apart and is not called waste.
- The same spend sits in several reviews (a click has a device, an hour and a location). Money is never added across reviews, nor across the account level and the inside campaigns level of one review.
- What can act on a finding inside a campaign depends on its bid strategy, from references/bid-adjustments.md (Official).

- For why it moved, the performance diagnosis skill splits the change by lever (how often ads showed, click through rate, cost per click, conversion rate), finds the day it started and lines up the account changes made before it. It reads this same file.
- Each review reads one export for one date range. Recent days can still gain conversions that are reported late.
- Segments are judged against this account's own numbers in the same file, not against a target or an industry figure.
- The same spend sits in several reviews (a click has a device, an hour and a location), so money is never added across reviews.
- Quality Score is a diagnostic reported for Search keywords, not a figure to optimize as an average (Official). It is never averaged here.
- An export shows how ads did, not why. Ad fatigue over time, the content of a page or a video, and policy detail need the account itself or the asset.
